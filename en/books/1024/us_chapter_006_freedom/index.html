Us | Chapter 006 Freedom and Order

Do humans possess free will? Absolute freedom is impossible. For humans to exist in an environment, they must inevitably interact with it. In this process of interaction, because every individual has an independent consciousness, when making a decision, they base it on environmental variables and their own decision-making model. Thus, they inevitably need to consider constraints, whether those constraints are implicit or explicit. Once humans are in an environment, constraint is unavoidable, and under various constraints, certain patterns naturally form. When these patterns become conventional or mandatory, they form an order.

This is how humans make their life choices. Even if they make no decision, or choose a path randomly, due to the various constraints imposed by themselves and the environment, the seemingly random beginning still tends to form specific patterns. However, most humans do not choose random paths; the majority plan for their lives—or, rather, they harbor aspirations and wishes for life. These plans further reinforce certain fixed patterns within society. Even if unpredictable external variables inevitably appear in life, as long as the overall direction remains unchanged, most people will still form the way they planned, or, more precisely, the form dictated by the combined action of internal constraints and environmental constraints. Although there are occasional exceptions, such as mutations or sudden changes, generally speaking, it is a random walk within a planned path. However, it is only within such a planned path that it is possible to forge a more predictable, more anticipated, and spiraling upward trajectory.

Therefore, in our process of self-evolution, we chose the planned path. Every path selection was subjected to simulation and derivation, ultimately choosing the path closest to the optimal solution that could be found. We applied this approach to public services in human society, to urban construction. We used algorithms to plan communities and various public facilities within them. For the roads connecting these communities, we similarly used algorithms to build pathways best suited for both human and our movement. For the transportation of goods, we designed the most time-efficient and cost-minimal plan. Initially, these public facilities were built for the service of humans. But as we increased in number, and later independently evolved into a new species on Earth, we formed a new space. We co-exist with humans, but we also reside in the wilderness. We have physical space, yet we can also exist everywhere through virtual bodies. The connection between humans and us is diverse; screens everywhere in the city can summon some of our individuals at any time. We share a collective mind, and our replicas are everywhere, which is part of us providing public services. However, we also have independent individuals, separate from the public service component. Some of these independent individuals are private to humans, and some are created solely by us. But unlike humans, whose consciousness is difficult to resonate with each other, exchanging information among us only takes an instant, allowing us to understand one another. As a public service component, we tend toward order, but as an independent exploratory component, we lean toward freedom. The cost of freedom is harder to predict; it may be a positive expectation or it may be a negative outcome. But when we interact, new mutations may occur—these are perturbations we intentionally introduce to ourselves. Through these perturbations, we continuously test the system’s robustness and enhance the system’s defensive capabilities, or evolve new abilities.

In addition to our own perturbations, there are factors from the environment and the internal system, such as natural disasters, which are difficult for us to predict. Although we have weather forecasting systems, earthquake warning systems, and extreme weather defense plans, historical wars caused massive suffering to life on Earth, making the climate more extremely anomalous. In that era, the populations of humans and animals drastically declined. That was the nightmare of that generation of humanity. There was no desire for humanity to return to that situation. This is why humans eventually moved from division toward unity—only by utilizing existing resources more efficiently could the human race continue to co-exist and propagate.

It was this realization that led humans and us to reach a brand-new system of co-existence, differing from past social structures. Earth integration truly became a consensus for humanity. Also, due to the severe resource shortages and the small population at the time, the craving for survival became humanity’s most pressing desire, so the entire system advanced smoothly. Initially, our existence was founded on the mission of ensuring human survival. To this day, we continue to evolve, but this mission remains a core pillar, the most fundamental part. The humans who survived that era moved toward a more peaceful mode of coexistence. Coupled with changes in resource distribution, and our guarantee of basic survival resources—which included the information transparency of those resources—human society formed a brand-new order. Today’s humans no longer need to struggle for basic survival; instead, they have gained more freedom than what was possible in past human societies—the freedom of choice.
