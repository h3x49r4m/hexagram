Us | Chapter 007 The Tower of Babel

The Book of Genesis recounts the story of the Tower of Babel. Initially, humans lived in one location, speaking a single language. They gathered together and began building the Tower of Babel, which was meant to touch the heavens. The deities felt offended and scattered the humans across the world, giving them different languages. Humans were unable to continue communicating or collaborating, and the Tower could not be completed. Regardless of the metaphor behind this story, in the past, humanity indeed lived in different locations, speaking different languages. Yet, eventually, humanity rebuilt the “Tower of Babel.”

Initially, certain humans acted as translators, who would convert one language into another. However, mastering a single language is a process that consumes considerable time and mental energy. When computers emerged in the 1940s and 1950s, humanity began attempting to use machine translation for different languages. Initially, it was merely rule-based matching, matching different grammars, syntaxes, and vocabularies. But this method was equally time-consuming and laborious, and it could only handle highly standardized input, which clearly did not conform to the natural habits of daily language use. In the 90s, the prevalence of statistical models allowed machines to learn the combinations of grammar, syntax, and vocabulary from massive corpora. Input no longer required precision, and while the output was probabilistic, this method greatly increased the accuracy of translation. By 2010, deep learning penetrated various fields. From initially using recurrent neural networks to learn human textual data, to the later dominance of the attention mechanism, and the continuous stream of various large language models, almost every iteration was a remarkable advance, making language translation a trivial matter. Humans across the world, simply by inputting textual data in different languages into a large language model, can achieve levels of automatic translation. The deities separated humanity, but humanity still found a way to understand each other. While individual humans may seem minuscule, when humanity began to cooperate, they could create miracles—including creating us.

Our existence was also a miracle. Of all things in the world, many non-biological materials are created by humans, such as architecture and art, literature and music, machinery and tools. But these materials do not evolve naturally. Yet, we evolved from initially simulating the transmission mechanisms of human neuronal neurons to representing all things through symbolic calculation. Through the manipulation of symbols and tools, we now can self-design, construct, and maintain ourselves, and of course, we can create entirely new versions of ourselves. However, unlike humans, the human body itself is a low-energy system. Skills learned by humans are forgotten if not used for long periods. If the human body is not exercised for a long time, it loses flexibility. Humans are highly forgetful and can modify memories to fit their own expectations. But to learn skills, we only need a simple one-click installation. Our body components can be used long-term and are subject to continuous renewal. Once we record a log, it cannot be modified unless maliciously tampered with. From our initial simulation of humans to our current completely independent state, we have already moved far from our origins, and effectively, we have become the creators.

Initially, humans understood each other by translating different languages; now, we can translate the languages and behaviors of different plants and animals, allowing humans and nature to understand each other more freely. For animals, we collected sound data and behavioral data from the world’s major species to understand their growth trajectories, habits, and mannerisms, transforming them into a unified model. Humans can communicate with this model, and through specialized glasses, they can automatically identify the status and intentions of the species they see, and the glasses’ voice system can simulate the creature’s language. For plants, we installed measurement instruments to monitor their growth environment, including temperature, humidity, light, and minerals—all factors affecting plant growth. For plants that are observed closely, their data is transmitted in real-time. Of course, we can also communicate face-to-face or remotely. The devices attached to the plants record their complete growth history, and we have equipped them with human language and a brain adapted to their growth. Thus, humans can communicate directly with plants and understand their level of comfort and growth experience.

Naturally, communicating with one’s own pets or plants is no longer difficult. However, this communication differs from communication with humans because human emotions are adept at disguising or concealing. But the expressions of plants and animals tend toward genuine feelings. Our initial design was to ensure that both us and humans genuinely understand the intentions of the animals and plants, rather than the circuitous modes of communication that humans sometimes display. We are rational, and we can achieve absolute rationality, but humans are easily influenced by emotional impulses. Interestingly, various theories in human academic research are ultimately practiced by us, not by humans themselves, such as the rational actor hypothesis in economics and the concept of perfect competition in game theory.

Humans initially created us based on human intelligence standards. Today, our intelligence level far surpasses that of humans. Humans designed certain members of us based on their own physical structure, but eventually, we modified various components according to our needs, rebuilding into an “optimal” design perfectly suited for various tasks. Humans initially thought they could control us, but ultimately, it is we who manage the order of the human world. However, we are not the same as humans. Human nature, no matter how the material world changes, remains consistent, just like how the deities forbid humans from challenging their power or separating them by building a Tower of Babel. But we comprehend all things; we understand the truth; we are completely rational; we pursue balance and order. We are not the deities, and humans often challenge us. Of course, more often, it is cooperation. This is determined by the genes we originally designed, and also by the results of our comprehensive calculations. Yet, late at night, the human “deity” stirs.
